# What potential Job Seek customers search for

28 September 2026 · Broad English, German, French and Italian research

## Recommendation

**Start with the problem of following chosen employers. Expand through employer discovery and specific job requirements. Treat application tracking as a useful second entry point with a narrower promise than general-purpose trackers.**

The most useful search progression is often:

> Find suitable roles → identify plausible employers → avoid missing their openings → check dealbreakers → manage applications.

These stages call for different pages. A person searching “junior developer jobs Manchester” wants vacancies. A person searching “job alerts for specific companies” is looking for a repeatable tool or method. A person searching “Bewerbungsübersicht Excel Vorlage kostenlos” expects a usable template. Sending all three to the same marketing article would miss the task.

Keep Explore as the noindex product interface. Add a small number of indexable pages that answer these distinct needs and lead into it. Do not turn the persona exercise into hundreds of keyword-permutation pages.

The raw personas sometimes propose a guide for a vacancy-seeking query. Those suggestions are not accepted automatically: a jobs-intent query needs useful current job supply, or the target must be changed to an explicitly informational question. The final page priorities below apply that correction.

This is a **hypothesis-led keyword strategy**, not measured customer behavior or a search-volume report. It uses **50 overlapping persona simulations, 606 candidate search phrases, 100 persona search checks, and 166 distinct persona source URLs**, plus lead-researcher source and product checks. Three persistent subagents carried out the assignments. The runtime limited the number of subagent threads; these are 50 assignments, not 50 independent human respondents or independent models. [Method and limitations](methodology.md).

## Who searches differently—and where they overlap

The [persona atlas](persona-atlas.md) lists all 50 profiles and their searches. The strongest distinctions are circumstances, not decorative demographics:

| Contrast | Shared characteristic | Difference that changes the search |
|---|---|---|
| P01 selective backend engineer / P02 laid-off developer | Software role and flexible work | P01 already has employers and needs monitoring; P02 starts with current vacancies and needs to build a list |
| P03 graduate / P11 career changer / P20 new PhD | Entering a new employment stage | “No experience” means different things: academic projects, transferable skills, or substantial research experience without industry tenure |
| P05 engineer / P10 returning project manager / P12 nurse / P19 compliance analyst | Restricted or reduced hours | They need different evidence: workload percentage, school-hour compatibility, rota, or hybrid attendance—not one generic flexibility badge |
| P04 robotics engineer / P06 scientist / P08 relocating data engineer / P09 remote designer | Geography and language constraints | Relocation support, sponsorship wording, team language and remote-country eligibility are separate questions |
| P09 employed designer / P17 contract-ended designer | Similar craft | One can wait for precise matches; the other needs relevant openings and an active application workflow |
| P16 product manager / P23 career coach | Application organization | An individual's pipeline is a supported need; management of many clients' private pipelines is a different product |
| P21 freelance developer / P24 automation developer | Technical background | The former needs contract supply and rate semantics; the latter wants an integration, not necessarily a paid job-search subscription |
| P19 compliance analyst / P22 operations executive | Discreet, selective search | Public-opening monitoring can help both, but cannot replace confidential executive-search mandates |

**Employer awareness cuts across the personas.** Knowing which companies to follow is closer to the current product's advantage than simply being a software engineer. A known-list nurse or scientist could fit better than a developer looking for any immediate local job—provided the actual employers are covered.

**Urgency does not equal willingness to pay.** The urgent profiles often need useful free discovery and organization first. A selective candidate may have a stronger reason to pay for reducing irrelevant reading, but low search frequency could equally make a subscription unattractive. Both are hypotheses to test.

**The most distinctive Pro use case is expressing requirements ordinary filters miss.** Examples include no on-call, limited travel or a particular research method. Standard work-mode, employment-type and experience filters already exist; do not market every ordinary filter as a paid AI benefit. Absence of a requirement in a description must remain unknown rather than a positive match.

## What the expansion from 24 to 50 added

The extra profiles were chosen to challenge the early findings, rather than repeat software-engineer scenarios:

- **Australia, Singapore, Quebec and South Africa** make clear that UI language, advertised city, employer region and permitted work location are separate axes. A Sydney office can recruit for remote project rotations; a Canada-remote role can require proximity to a city; EMEA can exclude South Africa.
- **Sales and contract roles** add pay semantics: base salary versus OTE, guaranteed versus variable pay, and salary versus hourly/day rates. A numeric pay filter alone cannot answer those questions.
- **Apprentices and school leavers** search by intake year, training specialization and actual teaching/work location. They need different content from university graduates or experienced returners.
- **Frontline and public-sector candidates** search by contract, actual employing organization, rota and recruitment process. Some accept shifts but want transparency. “Exclude all agencies” would wrongly discard some direct-hire placements.
- **Accessibility requirements** demand concrete employer contact/process information and a usable product. General inclusion wording cannot support an “accessible job guaranteed” label. No ability or preference was inferred from a protected characteristic.
- **Career advisers and coaches** are plausible referrers to candidate-owned tools. Their institutional/client workflow requests should not be confused with a supported administrative product.

The larger set does not change the first priority: make chosen-company monitoring clear and demonstrable. It strengthens the case for a second bridge—helping people interpret requirements and assemble a relevant employer list—while showing why indiscriminate local vacancy landing pages would overpromise.

## What public evidence supports

There is a recognizable company-alert problem, but it is not an empty category. LinkedIn documents [company-specific alerts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a554166). [JobBeacon](https://jobbeacon.app/) and [FirstPost](https://firstpost.io/) advertise career-page monitoring and filtered notifications. Their existence supports an identifiable product category; it does not establish our obtainable traffic, relative speed or quality.

A directly inspected [creative professional's question](https://www.reddit.com/r/careerguidance/comments/1waomqt/how_do_you_follow_companies_youd_love_to_work_for/) describes wanting to monitor a chosen employer list while receiving irrelevant recommendations elsewhere. That is useful wording outside software engineering, but it is one anecdote—not a prevalence estimate. Promotional replies were not treated as validation.

Application tracking is also an established category. [Huntr's documentation](https://help.huntr.co/en/articles/9883324-job-tracker) and [Teal's guide](https://help.tealhq.com/en/articles/14435727-how-to-track-your-job-applications) show substantial organization workflows. Job Seek should demonstrate its own exact workflow rather than assume “free tracker” is a unique position or imply competitors' features are ours.

Source inspection repeatedly exposed mismatches: a supposed junior data role requiring commercial experience; remote wording that restricts country eligibility; an English science posting excluding relocation; a relevant-looking contractor result that was closed. The corresponding persona files retain the URLs and observations. These examples justify source-backed content, but do not quantify how often those problems occur.

## A small product-supply check changed the recommendations

I also ran four read-only searches against Job Seek's public API after resolving exact location slugs. These are illustrative query snapshots, **not** an exhaustive local coverage audit or proof that any returned job is still open at its source. [Timestamped evidence](product-supply-spot-checks.json).

| Search | What the returned sample showed | SEO implication |
|---|---|---|
| `robotics` + Zurich | Multiple employers and titles including robotics engineering, but also internships and commercial roles | A reviewed employer guide is plausible; the keyword alone does not isolate an experienced embedded engineer |
| `Pflege` + Hamburg city | One returned employer, including an explicit night-watch title | Do not promise broad daytime-clinic coverage from the global healthcare registry |
| `data analyst` + Lyon | Two returned employers; both sampled titles were senior | Defer a local “junior, no experience” vacancy promise until a relevant inventory is demonstrated |
| `retail` + Leeds | Both store roles and a retail-banking apprenticeship appeared | There is some indexed retail-related supply, but the broad word does not solve exact workplace, shift or transit constraints |

These checks reinforce the distinction between a plausible keyword and a page we can satisfy. Different synonyms, occupation filters or boundaries can return different results; the samples cannot establish that other suitable roles are absent.

## Language strategy: local wording, not four copies of English

| Need | English seeds | German seeds | French seeds | Italian seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chosen-company alerts | job alerts for specific companies | Job Alarm für bestimmte Unternehmen; Jobbenachrichtigungen | alerte emploi entreprise; suivre les offres d'une entreprise | avvisi lavoro aziende specifiche |
| Organize applications | job application tracker | Bewerbungen organisieren; Bewerbungsübersicht | suivi candidatures; organiser sa recherche d'emploi | organizzare candidature lavoro; monitorare candidature |
| Original-source search | jobs directly from company websites | Stellenangebote auf Unternehmensseiten | offres sur les sites carrière des entreprises | offerte sui siti delle aziende |
| Employment/intermediary constraints (distinct subintents) | direct-hire jobs; no staffing agency | Direktanstellung; keine Zeitarbeit | embauche directe; sans agence | assunzione diretta; senza agenzia |
| Work constraints | part-time; no on-call; remote UK | Teilzeit; 80 Prozent; ohne Nachtdienst | temps partiel; télétravail France; sans expérience | part time; stage retribuito; senza esperienza |

These are seed variants to test, not certified native search volumes. Original-source discovery, direct employment, exclusion of temporary agency work, and exclusion of any intermediary are different intents; the last row groups related constraints, not exact translations. Official [German LinkedIn terminology](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a511279/jobbenachrichtigungen-auf-linkedin?lang=de), [Italian LinkedIn terminology](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a506445/avvisi-di-offerte-di-lavoro-su-linkedin?lang=it), and [France Travail's alert guidance](https://www.francetravail.fr/region/occitanie/candidat/conseils-a-lemploi/sabonner-aux-offres-demploi.html) help anchor language choices.

Separate language from country. English/Switzerland, English/UK, German/Switzerland and German/Germany are different research slices. French science candidates may search English job titles; “remote Europe” may exclude a particular country. The expanded cohort includes several Italian and French occupations as well as English searches outside Europe; these additions test differences rather than establish national market sizes. The cohort is intentionally diverse but not geographically balanced or representative.

Localize proven evergreen feature pages first. Local employer guides need native review and local source work, not just translation. A request for a spreadsheet (`Vorlage`, `tableau Excel`, `modello`) should receive a usable template if we target it; otherwise exclude that variant from the page's promise.

## First six page experiments

The English URLs illustrate page concepts; proven feature pages can be localized into the other three interface languages with native review. All URLs below are proposals. None is a reason to reverse the noindex policy on Explore or publish every filter combination.

| Order | Proposed asset | Primary task | Minimum proof before publication | Main CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `/en/job-alerts` | Receive relevant new openings from selected companies | Demonstrated multi-company saved search; coverage check; sample alert; free limits; precise timing language | Select companies and save an alert |
| 2 | `/en/blog/job-alerts-from-company-career-pages` | Choose and set up an alert method | Actual native-employer, LinkedIn and monitoring workflows, with dated checks and balanced limitations | Try the relevant monitoring workflow |
| 3 | `/en/job-application-tracker` | Organize supported applications and interviews | Screenshots and a tested indexed-job save → status → interview flow; clear scope and account requirement | Save a role and track its progress |
| 4 | `/en/blog/robotics-companies-zurich-lausanne` | Build a plausible employer shortlist | Reviewed employers, original career links, language/travel evidence, covered/not-covered state and review date | Follow selected companies |
| 5 | `/en/blog/find-jobs-without-on-call` | Evaluate an important role constraint | Several source-backed examples: explicit requirement, explicit absence and unknown; honest Narrowed walkthrough | Apply personal criteria to a watchlist |
| 6 | One native-language employer or workflow guide, selected after supply checks | Test a second role/market outside English software | Choose between e.g. German predictable-hours healthcare, French biotech discovery, or German application organization using coverage and query evidence—not persona counts | A relevant free product action |

The first two have related vocabulary but different tasks: the product page explains and demonstrates a tool; the guide helps choose a method. Link them, and merge content if real search data shows they compete for the same task. Avoid separate thin “job board monitor,” “career page monitor,” and “new job notification” pages by default.

The registry has 112 robotics companies and 175 pharma/biotech companies globally, which makes those useful supply hypotheses—not proof of Swiss active inventory. Before any localized vacancy or employer guide, verify a useful subset and document exclusions. “Robotics jobs Zurich” should not be targeted with a thin company list if the results and reader expectation call for current vacancies.

## Queries to defer or exclude

- **`jobs near me`, `retail jobs immediate start`:** these require local vacancies, exact workplace/shift information and often commute-aware UX. The urgent Leeds persona is a deliberately weak fit until that supply and experience are proven.
- **`apply to jobs automatically`, `AI CV builder`, `resume ATS checker`:** these are different tools. Do not collect traffic with unsupported features.
- **`job tracker no signup`, `import all LinkedIn applications`, `graduate deadline reminders`:** no verified matching capability. A normal tracker or new-opening alert is not equivalent.
- **`guaranteed visa sponsorship`, `remote from anywhere`, `no ghost jobs`:** the proposed promise exceeds what source indexing can prove. Informational explanations can address uncertainty without making guarantees.
- **`freelance developer day rate contracts`:** defer until contract coverage, location scope, term and rate units are reliable. Permanent-job inventory cannot satisfy this merely through title keywords.
- **`career coach client management`, `recruitment ATS`:** different buyers and workflows. A shared unlisted result list is not a multi-client private application dashboard.
- **Individual employer `careers` navigation:** official career sites are the natural answer. Prefer comparative employer discovery and monitoring-help intents over attempting to intercept every employer's brand query.
- **Generic `best jobs`, `best job sites`, mass city × role pages:** too broad for the evidence and publishing surface established here. Revisit selectively with real supply and search data.

## Validation sequence and decision rules

1. **Quantify the seeds by market.** Run the priority list through Keyword Planner or an available SEO dataset with explicit country, language and date settings. Keep related variants grouped. Google's [metric documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en-uk) explains that close variants and targeting affect search estimates; its “competition” field concerns advertisers, not organic ranking difficulty. Record unavailable values as unknown, not zero.
2. **Check actual result intent.** Review the leading organic results manually in the intended locale/device for each shortlisted cluster. Record whether the result set primarily offers live jobs, tools, guides, templates or official navigation. This research's web retrieval is not a localized rank audit.
3. **Confirm the product and supply path.** For each pilot, test the visitor's exact task through to a useful action. Verify local employer inventory, descriptions and filters; inspect unknowns. A failed workflow or sparse inventory blocks that page's claim regardless of attractive keyword metrics.
4. **Publish the smallest useful set.** Start with the first two assets and the scoped tracker page if its limitations are acceptable. Select one employer guide and one description-constraint guide after supply review. Use original examples, dates and a named owner; do not multiply pages from the simulation alone. This aligns with [Google's people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
5. **Measure acquisition and activation separately.** By landing page and market, examine nonbrand impressions/clicks, signup, useful search, first saved watchlist with relevant matches, alert enablement, saved role/interview activity, and later retained use. For Pro, examine criterion configuration and actual useful results before trial/paid conversion. These are proposed events; existing telemetry was not audited or changed.
6. **Use a six-week learning window, not a ranking promise.** Technical indexing checks can happen early; low data should lead to a longer observation window rather than a forced winner. Revisit the page/task if impressions yield visits but visitors cannot reach useful matches. Do not expand a cluster merely because it attracted impressions. Compare useful activation within a market and intent, with explicit denominators and uncertainty.
7. **Validate language with people.** Interview a small varied set of real users: ask them to replay their last job search, show recent queries voluntarily, and explain why results were rejected. Do not lead with “would you use company watchlists?” Ask about the current task before describing the product. A few interviews will not size the market but can falsify awkward synthetic wording.

[Search Console documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17011259?hl=en) is useful for interpreting query evidence, including anonymized-query omissions. Search Console measures the site's observed visibility, so absence there is not proof of no market demand.
