# Remaining blog keyword opportunities

28 September 2026 · English, German, French and Italian · post-marketing-release plan

## Recommendation

Build the next articles around **choosing employers** and **understanding the work hidden behind a job title**. Those questions lead naturally to a company watchlist or Narrowed. The alerts and tracker landing pages now address their main product searches; publishing more pages that restate those pitches would add little.

Start with seven briefs: Swiss robotics employers, jobs without on-call, remote-country eligibility, biotech employers in French-speaking Switzerland, German climate-tech employers, Italian marketing-versus-sales roles, and organizing applications/interview notes. This keeps the broad audience mix rather than turning the blog into a software-engineering-only publication.

The [65-row keyword backlog](blog-keyword-backlog.csv) maps candidate searches to **17 article briefs**, including one conditional template project. These are editorial priorities, not estimated traffic or guaranteed rankings. Each brief needs the original research specified below before publication.

## Evidence and limits

The baseline is the existing [50-persona research](report.md): 606 candidate queries, of which 100 exact queries were searched during that research. These are modeled personas, not 50 customer interviews. The backlog preserves query IDs for 58 rows and identifies seven additional phrases taken from the earlier priority list. Some queries legitimately appear in two briefs when their intent straddles a workflow and a comparison; they are not independent demand signals.

This review compared those candidates with the four existing blog topics and the new marketing pages, and spot-checked current primary sources and search-result formats. We do not have Search Console impressions/clicks, measured keyword volumes, difficulty, or conversion data. “Missing” means **no dedicated content satisfying the intent**, not proof that the site never appears for the query. Source/competitor observations below are examples, not a comprehensive or geographically controlled SERP ranking study.

## What not to make into another blog post

- **Already owned by a page:** `job alerts from specific companies`, `free job application tracker`, and `track job applications and interviews` should primarily strengthen their existing landing pages. Add supporting articles only for a different question.
- **Existing guide can answer it:** “LinkedIn alerts for only the companies I choose” can be an extension to the careers-page monitoring guide. A separate “LinkedIn alternative” article needs a hands-on, current comparison, not a restated sales pitch.
- **Transaction first:** “jobs near me,” “remote Python jobs UK,” city/role vacancy combinations, and apprenticeship-year searches principally need real listings. A blog directory can support discovery, but should not promise an inventory it does not maintain.
- **Capability mismatch:** automatic applications, importing every LinkedIn application, guaranteed sponsorship, no-signup private tracking, and deadline reminders are not suitable promises for this release.
- **Different audience:** `MCP job search server location salary filters` and `job search API updated since timestamp deleted jobs` belong in a developer tutorial/documentation plan, measured separately from job-seeker acquisition.
- **Too broad without original work:** generic interview tips, CV advice, “best job boards,” or “AI job search” listicles dilute the current advantage. Prefer source-checked employer and job-description research.

## Source checks that change the editorial plan

1. **Swiss robotics:** [ANYbotics’ own careers page](https://www.anybotics.com/about-us/careers/) and [its ATS board](https://jobs.lever.co/anybotics?workplaceType=onsite) provide employer/location/role evidence. They are starting sources for B01, not sufficient evidence for an entire regional directory.
2. **Remote eligibility:** a current [GitLab role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/gitlab/jobs/8623389002) explicitly distinguishes remote work from location-based eligibility. That supports B03’s reader problem. Check each advertised role rather than generalizing one employer’s policy to all remote work.
3. **French-speaking life sciences:** [Biopôle’s company directory](https://www.biopole.ch/biopole-community/biopole-companies/) and [its jobs page](https://www.biopole.ch/biopole/biopole-careers/jobs/) provide complementary discovery and vacancy sources. B04 should add role-family analysis and Job Seek coverage rather than copying a directory.
4. **Reduced hours:** the [Bundesagentur für Arbeit part-time search](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/jobsuche/suche?arbeitszeit=tz&suchbereich=jobs) is a live listing surface. B09 should answer the interpretation problem; a generic essay is a poor substitute for the transactional query.
5. **Apprenticeships:** [orientation.ch’s search guidance](https://www.orientation.ch/fr/chercher-une-place-dapprentissage) and [apprenticeship search](https://www.orientation.ch/fr/accueil-recherche/apprentissages) are primary starting points for Switzerland. A Job Seek article needs useful employer-specific work beyond these established resources.
6. **Spreadsheet intent:** current [MaxOfJob](https://maxofjob.com/job-application-tracker-spreadsheet) and [LifeShack](https://www.lifeshack.com/resources/job-search/free-job-application-tracker-template/) pages offer actual spreadsheet templates. That is format evidence for B16, not a claim about their market share or our ability to outrank them. Product comparisons still need hands-on checks.

## Publishing requirements and measurement

Every article should answer the query before asking readers to use Job Seek. Include a short decision table or annotated real descriptions, original sources, the last evidence-check date, and one relevant next action. Employer inventories should separate “covered by Job Seek” from “currently hiring.” Narrowed walkthroughs must test the exact displayed request and retain ambiguous/missed cases in the research notes.

Use the existing localized blog routes, canonical/hreflang handling, Article metadata and sitemap integration. Translate the reader problem and verify the market-specific evidence; do not turn France into Quebec or Germany into Switzerland by swapping place names. [Google’s multilingual guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/crawling/managing-multi-regional-sites) supports language-specific URLs and annotations. Google’s [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes original value; it does not prescribe a keyword density or reward publishing merely to appear fresh.

Follow the repository’s three-critic review process for actual blog drafts, then verify mobile readability, original links, company mentions, translated metadata and the complete CTA flow. This report is a backlog, not seventeen approved drafts.

Before scaling output, record a Search Console baseline when access is available: page/query/country impressions, clicks, CTR and position. At roughly 4 and 8 weeks after indexing, assess those alongside article → company/Explore clicks, watchlist creation, email opt-in, saved jobs and Narrowed activation where consented analytics supports them. Define events before claiming conversion improvements. Use results to adjust order, expand useful articles, or consolidate overlapping ones; no ranking outcome is guaranteed on that schedule.

Two housekeeping items belong beside the content plan: keep the May remote report explicitly historical unless the dataset is rerun; remove the welcome post’s unsupported “before the big aggregators” claim in its next reviewed revision. The blog authoring guide also still describes an English-only sitemap and a cadence-based demotion rule; bring those statements in line with the current localized implementation and Google’s guidance.
